## Jazz Course

The Jazz Course has three stages, each containing four modules and twelve short exercises:

- **Beginner:** intervals and triads, the major-key map, shell voicings and suspensions, then basic extensions and transposition.
- **Intermediate:** guide-tone motion, tritone substitution, chord-scale fluency, modal and quartal colors, upper structures, and pentatonic application.
- **Advanced:** altered harmony, advanced voicing movement, stride and bebop language, Latin-jazz cells, comping, and integrated improvisation practice.

The curriculum map is based on the concepts and chapter progression in Mark Levine's *The Jazz Piano Book*. All lesson names, theory summaries, objectives, instructions, and playable exercises in the app are original teaching material. The book itself is not bundled or reproduced; learners should use their own lawful copy for the referenced reading.

## Hanon Technique Course

The Hanon course contains 20 self-contained Part I lessons. Each lesson presents the complete public-domain pitch route in a scrollable engraved 2/4 grand staff with sixteenth-note beams, barlines, repeat signs, written ending values, and explicit left- and right-hand finger numbers on every note.

The verified catalog retains the written pitches. At practice-session time, Board Sensei raises the complete exercise by one octave when it still fits the C2–C7 piano, placing the left hand in the second visible octave; high-reaching exercises remain in their written register so every target stays playable. That one contextual route drives the score, keyboard highlights, note names, preview, fingering, and validation together.

Choosing **Both hands**, **Right hand**, or **Left hand** is silent: it clears the old playhead and scheduled preview generation, presents prompt one for the selected hand mode, and waits for **Hear full exercise**. The selected mode then projects through the engraved score, lesson route, piano targets, audible preview, validation policy, and completion count.

Exercise preview follows the written values at quarter-note BPM, performs the repeated body twice, and then plays the written ending. No external score is required.

The note routes were checked against the Mutopia Project's machine-readable Part I engraving, while render QA compares pitch contours, staff crossings, fingering, repeats, and endings with the supplied Schirmer pages; those page scans and editorial text are not bundled or reproduced. Parts II and III remain unavailable until their notes and fingerings can receive the same verification.
