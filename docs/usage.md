# Using Board Sensei

[Documentation index](README.md)

## Getting started

1. First launch opens a four-step onboarding flow: choose a practice goal and starting point, connect or skip a MIDI controller, then play middle C for an immediate first feedback moment. **Skip setup** remains available, and onboarding can be replayed later from Settings.
2. Open **Courses** and select **Jazz** or **Hanon Technique**. Jazz initially opens at the level selected during onboarding, while Hanon currently contains the verified Part I exercises.
3. Choose Beginner, Intermediate, or Advanced, expand a chapter, and select a lesson.
4. The lesson opens directly in the unified class workspace. Its scrollable left guide contains the complete explanation, purpose, preparation plan, listening focus, success criterion, narration, exercise preview, and route. Jazz uses its adaptive prompt staff and exact chord rules; Hanon uses its complete written score, fingering, hand selection, and measure-range controls.
5. Drag the divider between the guide and instrument workspace left or right to choose how much room each side receives.

## Exercise playback

1. In a Jazz lesson, use the **Key** menu beside the metronome to choose any of the 12 tonal centers. The complete exercise is transposed from its C-relative source: staff notation and accidentals, piano targets, note names, route, preview, and MIDI validation all change together. The choice is retained for the next Jazz lesson and never affects Hanon.
2. Use the class transport to arm the metronome, set its independent volume, choose a common time signature, and adjust tempo from 50–180 BPM. Arming the metronome is silent; clicks sound only during exercise playback or the explicit scored practice loop.
3. Use **Hear full exercise** for one complete demonstration. Toggle the adjacent repeat icon before or during playback to cycle that demonstration continuously. The cycle follows the selected tempo, pads each repetition to a complete measure, and restarts on beat one while the metronome, staff, piano, and lesson route remain synchronized without changing the score.
4. Press **Space** while a demonstration or loop is running to stop it. Space is stop-only and does nothing while playback is idle.

## Narration and instruments

1. Choose **Listen** in Lesson narration to hear the bundled studio recording. If a current recording is unavailable, the player identifies and uses the Mac system voice as a fallback. It supports pause, resume, and stop and ends automatically when you change lessons or leave class mode.
2. Select the instrument icon beneath the output meter to choose a grand piano, electric piano, synth lead, or synth pad. The selection is saved and applies to live playing, demonstrations, and loops.

## Practice and progress

1. Play each highlighted Jazz note or exact chord voicing, or each fresh Hanon hand pair, with a MIDI keyboard or the on-screen five-octave piano. Hanon pairs allow 120 ms between hands but must be re-articulated for every written step. Jazz chord prompts also provide **Play shown chord** for hardware-free practice.
2. Use **Previous** and **Next** at the bottom of the class guide, or open the chapter menu to jump directly to any lesson in the current chapter.
3. Completing a pass shows a compact inline result strip without covering the staff or piano. Choose **Practise again**, start **Loop with metronome** for automatically repeated scored passes aligned to beat one, or continue to the next lesson only when comfortable. Lesson records are saved automatically: entry marks a lesson **Introduced**, one completion marks it **Practised**, and three completions with a best accuracy of at least 90% mark it **Mastered**.

## Settings and MIDI Lab

1. A practice-day rhythm updates only on meaningful lesson completion. It, completion sounds, and animated celebrations can each be disabled in Settings; macOS Reduce Motion also suppresses energetic celebration motion.
2. Open **Settings** to change the learning profile, replay onboarding, configure game-feel preferences, or choose the full-key colors used for expected and pressed/preview notes.
3. Open **MIDI Lab** to select a device, inspect normalized events, or recover from a stuck note with **Panic**. Once connected, the lab shows a clean, read-only five-octave keyboard that mirrors incoming note presses without lesson markings or computer-key labels.

See [courses](courses.md) for curriculum and Hanon score details.
